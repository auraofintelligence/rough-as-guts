Young crew

Funny, practical picture guides for the everyday things nobody is born knowing.

Have a lookMake a new page
Small moves. More independence. A decent laugh.

A few useful clues for doing more of life yourself.

School, friends, home, messages, work experience and getting yourself somewhere on time can pile up quickly. Young Crew is a proposed, school-appropriate part of Rough as Guts, built around situations young people recognise and skills they can actually use.

You can learn this stuff

Being unsure is a normal place to begin. A picture can make an awkward moment easier to talk about: an overdue message, a forgotten item, a room that got away from you or a job you have never tried before.

  • Show the situation clearly.
  • Offer a manageable next action.
  • Leave room to try again.
  • Keep the person’s dignity intact.

Everyday independence

The proposed guides focus on small skills that make a day easier: getting ready, looking after your things, preparing simple food, cleaning up and treating shared spaces with care.

  • Check the bag before leaving.
  • Put an essential item somewhere you can find it.
  • Learn a simple meal and clean up afterwards.
  • Keep clothes, sports gear and personal hygiene on the radar.
  • Ask for help with something unfamiliar.

Friends, messages and finding your words

A useful joke can open a conversation about listening, handling disagreement, respecting boundaries or pausing before sending a message. Young Crew material should sound like it understands the situation, with language a young person can use.

  • Give people a chance to finish speaking.
  • Check what someone means before building a whole story around it.
  • Pause when a reply is coming from anger.
  • Find a trusted adult when a problem feels too big to carry alone.

Moving, travelling and trying new things

Exercise, everyday journeys and work experience offer plenty of visual humour. The proposed pages explore preparation, realistic expectations and how to ask a good question when everyone else seems to know what is happening.

  • Know where you are going and how you are getting home.
  • Bring the essentials for the activity.
  • Learn a new task from someone who can show you.
  • Choose an achievable start and build confidence through practice.

Made to read together

A parent, teacher, youth worker, coach or young person could use a single page to start an ordinary conversation. The humour needs to work for the reader, with characters who have strengths, make mistakes and get another go.

  • School-appropriate language and situations.
  • Different kinds of young people with a fair share of the good ideas.
  • Clear pictures with one useful point.
  • Young Crew concepts are in development and need feedback from young readers and the adults supporting them.

A taste of the rough stuff

Sample ideas in development. These are new concepts, separate from the source deck.

01

The Bag That Was Ready Yesterday

The bag is zipped. The shoes are on. The bus is coming.

Your lunch has chosen to stay on the bench.

Picture idea: A teenager at the front door points proudly to a packed bag while a lunchbox on the kitchen bench holds a tiny sign saying WAIT.

Concept in development
02

The Sports Bag Wildlife Reserve

Something in the bag has been there since last week.

Air the gear before it starts charging rent.

Picture idea: Two teenagers open a sports bag; cartoon smell lines form a tiny imaginary creature while one holds a laundry basket.

Concept in development
03

The Reply You Wrote While Furious

A very long message. Several capital letters. A dramatic ending.

Save the draft. Take a breath. Read it again.

Picture idea: A teenager’s phone displays a comically oversized message bubble while a friend offers a pause button on a small card.

Concept in development

Life skills. A bit of dignity.

Young Crew is intended for high-school-age readers, including young people who need more support. The page ideas aim for recognition, practical next steps and humour without making a kid’s circumstances the joke.
