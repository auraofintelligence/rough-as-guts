For the women

From the overloaded handbag to the hopeful dog, the joke opens the door to more care, confidence and everyday ease.

Get a tasteInside the edition
Joyful responsible abundance.

A laugh, a change and room to enjoy yourself.

These proposed pages give different women room to recognise a familiar habit and try another way. A friend might read one with you, share their own version or lend a hand. The payoff is a cleaner shared kitchen, a finished project or a night out that ends well. Joy has room to grow when responsibility is shared.

A laugh with somewhere to go

Draft ideas to try with readers. Each shows a familiar habit, a different response and something worthwhile on the other side.

01

The Chair Returns to Active Seating

Clean clothes, worn-once clothes and clothes awaiting a decision have taken the chair out of service. She catches the joke when a friend has nowhere to sit.

Chair management is delighted to announce the return of sitting.

A way forward

Sort the clothes into what needs washing and what can be put away, then keep seating and shared walkways available for their intended use.

What it makes room for

The space becomes easier to use and friends can spend their time together rather than moving the same pile.

Picture idea: A chair buried in clothes has acquired an absurd little mailbox. The woman sorts the clothes into laundry and suitable storage, removes the mailbox and invites her friend to sit with her. The last panel gives both people a comfortable place to enjoy their visit.

Concept for discussion
02

Packing for the Trip You Are Actually Taking

Her imagined beach, hiking and fancy-dinner selves are all adding luggage. The person who has to carry it asks for a meeting.

The committee has agreed to take the person and leave several hypothetical holidays at home.

A way forward

Pack for the actual activities and expected conditions, check the essentials and shared arrangements, and choose luggage you can manage appropriately.

What it makes room for

Travel preparation becomes more dependable and comfortable, with attention left for the people and places you are going to enjoy.

Picture idea: Comic imagined holiday selves compete to fill a suitcase. The traveller checks the actual plans, conditions and essentials with a friend, makes a suitable packing list and repacks. In the final scene she can manage her luggage and help with shared trip preparations.

Concept for discussion
03

The Workout Collection Goes Outside

She has saved enough exercise videos for an impressive opening presentation. Then she asks a mate to join her in an activity they can both enjoy.

The bookmark button has earned a rest. The rest of us are going for a walk.

A way forward

Choose movement that suits your ability and circumstances, learn anything unfamiliar from a suitable person and make room to enjoy it regularly with or without company.

What it makes room for

Wellbeing becomes something lived and supported, with less pressure to perform an ideal version of yourself.

Picture idea: A woman surveys a comically long ribbon of saved workout videos. She puts the phone aside, agrees an appropriate activity with a friend and joins in. The ending shows them enjoying the outing rather than admiring new gear or comparing bodies.

Concept for discussion

The Handbag Mining Lease Can Close Early

She can produce pliers and emergency earrings, but the house key requires a full excavation. The comic discovery gives her a reason to clear out food, return loose items to useful places and give the essentials a home. She gets to be resourceful without carrying last month's lunch as evidence.

  • The bag can carry supplies without becoming long-term food storage.
  • A key pocket is allowed to contain keys.
  • The excavation crew finishes early enough to enjoy the coffee.

A way forward

Empty out rubbish and food, keep only what is useful for the day and put keys, phone and other essentials in consistent places. Clean the bag as its care instructions allow.

What it makes room for

Finding things takes less effort, food stays where it belongs and being prepared feels lighter.

Picture brief for this chapter

A woman and a friend survey the absurd contents of an overloaded handbag. She clears out rubbish and old food, sorts the essentials into useful places and puts the keys into a consistent pocket. The final panel shows her finding them easily as both friends leave for a walk.

The Op-Shop Rescue Gets a Proper Home

She can see the potential in the cabinet. Everyone else can see the blocked garage. The page gives that enthusiasm somewhere to go: choose a project with a real use, check what it needs and finish it before filling the next trailer. The final reveal is furniture in use, with room for the people who live there.

  • "It only needs a quick sand" can come with an honest look at the work.
  • The bargain includes somewhere suitable to put it.
  • A finished chair is allowed to hold a visitor.

A way forward

Check the condition, work required, space and intended use before bringing something home. Agree about shared space, get suitable help for unfamiliar repairs and finish, pass on or properly dispose of stalled projects.

What it makes room for

Reuse becomes useful rather than a burden, shared areas remain accessible and the pleasure of making something is easier to enjoy.

Picture brief for this chapter

A woman stands beside a growing collection of unfinished second-hand finds. She and a household member choose one useful, suitable project and clear the others through agreed reuse or disposal. The final panel shows the finished cabinet in use and a clear garage walkway.

The Grand Entrance Includes a Good Way Home

She arrives ready for a marvellous night. The imagined departure involves a chip box full of shoes and a cake balanced on a raffle ticket. The story changes before that happens: a suitable way home, footwear she can manage and a container for anything she is taking. Preparation gives the good night somewhere decent to land.

  • The emergency flats are more useful on this side of the front door.
  • A raffle ticket has limited qualifications as a cake carrier.
  • A good exit can leave the host with fond memories and their crockery.

A way forward

Arrange a suitable way home, take what you need to be comfortable and check with the host before taking food or items away. Help leave the venue ready for others.

What it makes room for

Enjoyment comes with less avoidable stress, friends can look out for each other and hospitality stays a pleasure.

Picture brief for this chapter

A woman imagines a comically chaotic departure from a bowls-club celebration. She checks her arrangements before leaving, packs suitable footwear and takes a proper food container if needed. The final panel shows friends leaving considerately on their arranged ride while helping the host clear the table.

The Dog Retires From Dishwashing

The dog looks delighted with his kitchen promotion. She catches herself treating a lick as a wash and laughs at how low the entry requirements were. Then she takes the plate back, washes the people’s crockery properly and settles the dog at his own bowl outside the kitchen. The dog remains adored, with a job suited to a dog.

  • An enthusiastic tongue is not washing-up equipment.
  • The dog's bowl can be his own dining room.
  • His new position is Waiting Hopefully, on the floor.

A way forward

Keep pets and their feeding items out of the kitchen and food preparation area. Wash pet items separately from people’s cooking and eating items, wash people’s dishes properly and wash your hands after handling pets or their food, bowls and mess.

What it makes room for

Care for the pet and care for the people sharing the kitchen work together. Guests can relax and meals become more welcoming.

Picture brief for this chapter

Three panels: a woman notices the dog licking a people’s plate and catches the absurdity; she takes the dog and his feeding items out of the kitchen, washes the people’s plate properly and washes her hands after the pet clean-up; friends and family enjoy a clean table while the dog eats from his own bowl outside the kitchen and food preparation area.
