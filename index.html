Joyful responsible abundance.

Have a laugh. Leave with a better way.

Rough as Guts starts with habits we can recognise in ourselves. The familiar excuse gets the laugh, then the person gets a way to do things differently. The aim is joyful responsible abundance: more health, capability, trust, time and shared enjoyment.

A laugh with somewhere to go

Draft ideas to try with readers. Each shows a familiar habit, a different response and something worthwhile on the other side.

01

The Mugs Are Coming Home

The bedside table has become an unofficial café. The cups have outlasted several menus.

The café has closed. The owner is doing the washing-up.

A way forward

Take your used mugs back, wash them and leave shared spaces ready for the next person. Agree a routine that everyone contributes to.

What it makes room for

A cleaner place to eat and drink, less work left for someone else and a space you can enjoy.

Picture idea: Three beats: a collection of abandoned mugs; the same person carries and washes them; the clear table holds a book and one fresh cup while they relax.

Draft to try with readers
02

Packed for the Trip You Are Taking

The weekend bag has two backup jackets and no toothbrush.

The spare expedition has been cancelled. The toothbrush made the team.

A way forward

Check the trip, the essentials and what you can carry before packing. Use a list you can change after the journey.

What it makes room for

Less hauling, fewer forgotten necessities and more time to enjoy being away.

Picture idea: A traveller checks the actual trip needs, removes surplus gear and packs toiletries; the final scene is a relaxed arrival with a manageable bag.

Draft to try with readers
03

The Job That Actually Ends

A loose cupboard handle is attracting a renovation speech.

One right screw. A whole Saturday still available.

A way forward

Work out the actual problem before taking things apart. Use suitable instructions or ask someone who knows, and recognise when the job needs a qualified person.

What it makes room for

A working cupboard, less waste and a weekend that still contains leisure.

Picture idea: An adult pauses before dismantling the cupboard, checks the simple repair with a knowledgeable mate, completes it and joins friends outside. No electrical or structural work.

Draft to try with readers

A gift that offers a hand

Give a guide to a mate, partner or yourself as an invitation to try something together. A shared laugh can open a conversation about the mess, the borrowed gear or the way we talk to each other. Offer help and leave room for a reply.

  • Read the page together instead of using it to embarrass someone.
  • Talk about the behaviour and what would help.
  • Share the work of making the change.

A way forward

Choose a habit you are willing to work on too. Agree what each person will do, then notice the effort when things improve.

What it makes room for

Less resentment and more room for people to enjoy being together.

Different lives. The same dignity.

Men’s, women’s and Young Crew editions use different situations and characters. The opportunity to learn belongs to everyone. Worksite material also asks what training, equipment, supervision and work design need to improve, rather than blaming the person nearest the problem.

  • Men: finishing jobs, sharing care, listening and returning favours.
  • Women: hygiene, boundaries, making space and enjoying life with less chaos.
  • Young Crew: practical independence, support and permission to learn a different way.
  • Worksites: safe work, speaking up and a crew that acts on concerns.

Joyful responsible abundance

The reward is a fuller life: health to enjoy it, skills to take part, relationships that hold up and time left for the people and things we love. Responsibility makes room for that enjoyment. The humour can be blunt and Australian while still being on the reader’s side.

  • Laugh at the excuse, then help the person move beyond it.
  • Celebrate asking, learning, repairing and contributing.
  • Keep pleasure, play and belonging in the final picture.

What else could it become?

Books, posters, cartoons, animation and club characters can carry the same purpose: a laugh, a useful change and something worth enjoying.
