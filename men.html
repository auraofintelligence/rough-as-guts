MEN'S EDITION · IN DEVELOPMENT

For the blokes

A laugh at the familiar excuse, followed by a way to finish the job, share the load and look after the people around you.

Get a tasteInside the edition
Joyful responsible abundance.

More clue. More time for the good stuff.

A useful gift says, "Let's have a laugh and make this easier." These proposed pages follow different blokes recognising a habit, trying another way and getting something worthwhile back: trust, a calmer household, a job properly finished or an afternoon with their mates. The person in the joke gets to be the person who changes it.

A laugh with somewhere to go

Draft ideas to try with readers. Each shows a familiar habit, a different response and something worthwhile on the other side.

01

The Date Commentary Channel Finds an Interviewer

He has explained his old car, his new car and several developments in trailer reversing. Then he notices he has barely asked a question.

Breaking news: there is another person on this date.

A way forward

Ask about the other person's interests, listen to the answer and make room for them to choose what they share. Respect their boundaries and interest.

What it makes room for

The meeting becomes a conversation both people can enjoy, with less pressure to perform or impress.

Picture idea: Three comic beats: the man's stories fill a large speech bubble; he catches himself and asks an open question; both adults are talking and listening, with the empty boast bubble folded into a tiny paper aeroplane beside his plate.

Concept for discussion
02

Dad Packed the Bag, Then Checked With the Kid

The day bag has supplies for an expedition. The child quietly holds up the missing shoes.

Expedition control has accepted a late report from the actual passenger.

A way forward

Check the activity and essentials together, include the child's needs and perspective, then confirm the travel and return plan with the responsible adults.

What it makes room for

The adult shares the preparation properly and the child gets practice contributing rather than being managed around.

Picture idea: A parent proudly presents an overprepared day bag. The child points to their shoes and a simple activity list. Together they check what is needed, adjust the bag and head out smiling.

Concept for discussion
03

The Gym Opening Ceremony Finally Starts the Session

New shoes, new watch, new bottle and a ribbon-cutting ceremony. His mate applauds, then points to the activity they agreed to do.

The opening ceremony has concluded. Participants may now participate.

A way forward

Choose an activity that suits your current ability, learn how to do it appropriately and make an arrangement you can return to. Ask for suitable guidance when unsure.

What it makes room for

Movement becomes something to enjoy and practise, with encouragement that lasts beyond buying the gear.

Picture idea: A bloke makes a grand launch of an exercise mat. In the next panel he puts the ceremony props aside and joins a mate in an ordinary, appropriate activity they know how to do. The final panel shows them enjoying the outing together.

Concept for discussion

The Five-Minute Job Can Have an Ending

He went in to fix a loose handle. Now the cupboard is in several suburbs of the kitchen and he is considering a site office. The turn comes when he stops expanding the job, checks the method and gets the original repair finished. The final picture gives the household its kitchen back and him a reason to put the kettle on.

  • The handle asked for help. The entire kitchen did not request redevelopment.
  • A job can pause for a question before it becomes a long weekend.
  • Put the tools away and leave the space ready for the next person.

A way forward

Agree what needs fixing, check that you know how to do it safely, get suitable help where needed, then finish and clear the work area before adding another job.

What it makes room for

The repair becomes useful to everyone, shared space stays usable and the free time at the end belongs to the household too.

Picture brief for this chapter

Three panels: a bloke surveys the unnecessary cupboard demolition; he puts the right instructions beside the original loose handle and gets help with an unfamiliar part; he finishes the repair, clears the bench and joins the household for a cuppa.

The Fridge Can Retire From Archaeology

Every container has a lid. Very few have an explanation. Instead of appointing a family member Chief Investigator, he owns the clean-out and the next meal plan. Labels, storage directions and a clear shelf replace the guessing game. The joke can end with an ordinary lunch being easy to find, which is a decent result for an expedition.

  • "Tuesday's leftovers" could use a date and an owner's name.
  • An empty milk carton is information for the shopping list, not a fridge ornament.
  • A sniff is not a substitute for the food's storage and date guidance.

A way forward

Take responsibility for your food: label and date prepared items, follow their storage guidance, deal with food that should no longer be kept and replace empty packaging with a shopping-list entry.

What it makes room for

People can find their food, share the fridge considerately and spend less time guessing or cleaning up someone else's mystery.

Picture brief for this chapter

Three panels: a man confronts a fridge full of anonymous containers; he checks labels and storage guidance, deals with unsuitable food and cleans the shelf; he packs a clearly labelled lunch and adds milk to the shared shopping list.

The Apology Can Lose Its Footnotes

He has prepared an explanation involving traffic, weather and what his mate would have done. Then he notices the person waiting for him to own the actual mistake. The whiteboard folds away. He says what he did, listens and offers a repair he can follow through on. The humour comes from retiring the defence team, not from making the hurt person accept it.

  • "I stuffed that up" can travel without a supporting slide deck.
  • Listen to the effect before presenting the excuse.
  • A promised repair needs to appear in the next scene.

A way forward

Name what you did and its effect, apologise without shifting the blame, listen and agree a repair you can actually carry out. Give the other person room to respond.

What it makes room for

Responsibility becomes visible, the same argument has less fuel and trust has something practical to grow from.

Picture brief for this chapter

A man begins an apology beside an enormous explanation chart. In the next panel he rolls it up and listens. In the final panel he carries out the agreed repair while the other adult has space to respond in their own time.

The Borrowed Drill Gets to Go Home

The drill has been at his place long enough to know the neighbours. Its owner would quite like a reunion. Rather than celebrating the long loan, the page follows him finding the charger, checking the borrowed kit and arranging its return. His mate's surprise at seeing the whole lot together can carry the last laugh.

  • The case, the tool and the charger were originally a group booking.
  • Mention damage while you are still the person returning it.
  • The next favour is easier when the previous one came home.

A way forward

Ask before borrowing, agree when it will come back, return the complete kit in suitable condition and tell the owner promptly about any damage or problem.

What it makes room for

The owner can use their own gear, awkward chasing is avoided and helping each other remains enjoyable.

Picture brief for this chapter

A drill appears in an absurdly affectionate family-photo setup. Its borrower then gathers the charger and accessories, speaks honestly to the owner and returns the complete kit. Both mates share a cuppa with the tool back on its proper shelf.

The Sideline Has Room for a Supporter

He arrived at junior sport with a tactical briefing worthy of a national final. His child mainly hoped he would watch. When he catches himself, the tactics board goes back in the car. He encourages the players, leaves coaching to the coach and asks what the child enjoyed. His enthusiasm finally has a job everybody can live with.

  • The coaching position has already been filled.
  • "I liked watching you play" does not require a performance review.
  • There are cones to collect if the tactical department needs a task.

A way forward

Check what support the young player wants, follow the club's spectator expectations, encourage effort respectfully and offer practical help to the volunteers.

What it makes room for

Children get room to enjoy learning, volunteers can do their jobs and sport becomes a shared pleasure.

Picture brief for this chapter

A dad begins beside an oversized tactics board. His child points to the actual coach, and Dad catches the joke. He puts the board away, cheers respectfully and helps collect the cones before walking home listening to the child's story.
