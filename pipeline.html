Books, cartoons & other possibilities

01

Illustrated books and themed collections

Gift picture books

The Museum of Things I'll Fix: a guided tour of temporary repairs that have become family heirlooms. Each spread introduces another magnificent exhibit and the person defending it.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
Someone you love treats every household job as an optional side quest.
Possible setting
Gift-shop, bookshop, club stall, roadhouse shelf or direct-order edition.
Sample version
A cover and three illustrated spreads from The Museum of Things I'll Fix.
02

Pocket booklets, zines and fold-out guides

Small guides for very specific disasters

Your First Week Feeding Yourself: a character discovers that a cupboard full of sauces is a recipe for owning sauces. One spread follows a meal from shopping list to the final washing-up.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
A character leaves home and discovers a few jobs that used to happen without their involvement.
Possible setting
Gift packs, local shops, camp welcome packs, library activities or leaving-home presents.
Sample version
A folded booklet following one meal from an empty fridge to the washing-up.
03

Posters and noticeboard series

The fridge has lodged a complaint

A mock statement from the fridge: 'I can keep it cold. I can't make it yours.' A cutaway scene shows labelled lunch boxes, a mystery tub and a worker trying to deny a very familiar casserole dish.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
The shared fridge is full, nobody owns the smell and everybody owns the good milk.
Possible setting
Camp noticeboards, club kitchens, lunchrooms, sheds and community halls.
Sample version
A shared-fridge poster with labelled lunch boxes, a mystery tub and the fridge's written complaint.
04

Newsletter column and recurring email feature

The Crib Room Correspondent

The kettle writes to management about being left empty. Next issue, the broom responds to allegations that it has never been seen during a shift.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
An everyday object gets a right of reply.
Possible setting
Club, workplace, camp, association or community newsletters.
Sample version
An illustrated letter from the kettle, followed by a reply from the broom in the next issue.
05

Magazine, seasonal annual or mini newspaper

The Rough as Guts Quarterly

One issue could contain a shed cartoon, a camp-life letter, a 'spot the pointless extra step' puzzle, a character interview and the Lost Property Awards.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
A collection mixing cartoons, fictional letters, puzzles and illustrated stories.
Possible setting
Local-shop counters, club reading piles, ag-show stalls or a subscription edition.
Sample version
A sample issue with a shed cartoon, a camp-life letter, a puzzle and the Lost Property Awards.
06

Single-panel newspaper cartoons and four-panel strips

Bush Telegraph Toons

A pothole gets its own office, waiting room and retirement plaque while the road crew tries to find its original job number. The subject is bureaucracy, with room for a different local gripe each week.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
A small-town inconvenience becomes an absurd public institution.
Possible setting
Country newspapers, community news sheets, town newsletters and local websites.
Sample version
A single-panel pothole cartoon and a four-panel strip about its retirement ceremony.
07

Caption jokes, illustrated one-liners and share cards

The Short Yarn Department

A worker presents an elaborate diagram of where the missing broom could be. Behind him, it is holding the door open.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
An elaborate explanation for an obviously avoidable mess.
Possible setting
Newsletter fillers, club screens, social feeds, calendars and printed cards.
Sample version
An illustrated broom joke with alternative captions on separate cards.
08

Comedy sketches, short scripts and performed video

The Five-Minute Job

Two mates begin with a loose cupboard handle. Each helpful arrival brings a larger tool and a longer explanation. The quiet person who fetched the right screw finishes the job while the committee is still discussing access.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
A tiny repair attracts a committee and turns into a major operation.
Possible setting
Hall gigs, club nights, short video series and camp entertainment.
Sample version
A short script for the cupboard-handle repair, with entrances for each increasingly helpful visitor.
09

Sticker sheets, labels, magnets and removable decals

Small signs with a point

A dishwasher sticker reads 'A cupboard with consequences' above a tiny character carefully stacking a plate. A shared-food label says 'This has an owner. Apparently that's me.'

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
A small joke attached to the cupboard, appliance or lunch box involved.
Possible setting
Club kitchens, households, lunch boxes, sheds, welcome packs and gift bundles.
Sample version
A sheet of dishwasher, lunch-box and shared-fridge labels with small character illustrations.
10

Task drawings and visual companions to reviewed safe work method statements

Illustrated support for the actual SWMS

A matching task sheet drawn from the equipment, location and sequence in a supplied, reviewed SWMS. The responsible task team checks the depicted controls and steps before using it in their material.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
Illustrations of a specific task, linked to its written sequence and actual equipment.
Possible setting
Task-specific work documents and site training material, with the responsible organisation and reviewers.
Sample version
A task drawing beside its supplied SWMS, with source details and reviewer notes.
Safe Work Australia: SWMS guidance

Safe Work Australia's guidance describes SWMS for high risk construction work. This proposal is illustration support tied to the specific work and its review process.

11

Short animation, loops and recurring episodes

When the job is “finished”

Possible scene: the cook announces “Dinner’s done” over one plate and every saucepan in the house. Would the reveal carry the joke, or does it need a reply from the person facing the washing-up?

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
Someone declares a job finished while the part they dislike is left for somebody else.
Possible setting
Short-video channels, club screens, event displays and companion content for books.
Sample version
Try a silent reveal of the kitchen, then a version with the reply “You missed a bit.” Compare whether the joke is clear and whether either version feels like a familiar household moment.

Tea towels, mugs, aprons, shirts, calendars and shed prints

Objects that carry the joke

A tea towel shows the Workload Forecast: dishes gathering over the sink, clearing after someone actually dries them. An apron gives the cook a dramatic title beside a very modest meal.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
A useful household object comments on the job it keeps being denied.
Possible setting
Gift stalls, club fundraisers, roadhouses, small shops and direct-order bundles.
Sample version
A Workload Forecast tea towel mock-up, with a matching sink-side print.
13

Sports-club mascots, match-day cartoons and junior activity sheets

Give the club a larrikin

A fictional galah mascot announces itself as Head of Encouragement and Avoiding the Washing-Up. It can star in a match-day strip, a volunteer thank-you, a gear-return reminder and a junior colouring page.

Premise, setting & sample

Sample version
An original galah character sheet, a match-day cartoon and a gear-return notice.

17

Short audio yarns, radio-ready sketches and narrated cartoons

“Just a quick favour”

Possible phone sketch: a mate asks to borrow the ute, then keeps adding furniture to the list. The other person asks, “Am I helping you move, or do I live with you now?” Does the growing request offer enough to play with?

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
A request to pick up a couple of things grows until it sounds like a house move.
Possible setting
Community-radio submissions, club audio, podcasts and accessible narrated editions.
Sample version
Read it aloud with the requests delivered casually, then with increasingly obvious attempts to hide the scale of the favour. Which version, if either, gives the other person a useful comic response?

Premise, setting & sample

Club tables, family gatherings, camp recreation rooms and gift packs.
Sample version
A printable pack of picture cards, excuse cards and a one-page set of game rules.
COUNTRY, OUTBACK & A BIT SIDEWAYS

Open the other shed.

A few stranger concepts for ag shows, country halls, roadhouses, camps and clubs.

The Cubicle Gazette

A tiny changing newspaper for the back of the door, complete with a headline, one cartoon and an absurd classified ad.

A serious broadsheet front page reporting that the last clean mug has been sighted near the sink.

Setting & sample

Country-show toilets, club bathrooms and camp amenities, with permission from the venue.

A door-sized newspaper sheet with one cartoon, a mock headline and a classified ad.

The Lost Property Royal Show

Abandoned everyday objects receive the treatment of prize livestock, complete with judging notes and ribbons.

A magnificent unmatched glove under a Champion ribbon; a line of hopeful coffee mugs waits to be judged.

Setting & sample

An ag-show display or club foyer.

Removable illustrated award cards and a miniature display of fictional prize-winning lost property.

The Corrugated Comic

A comic designed across a series of upright corrugated panels, so readers walk along the story.

A three-stage attempt to close a stubborn gate, ending with the quiet discovery that somebody stacked timber behind it.

Setting & sample

A show stand, shed exhibition or temporary community-art display.

A paper model of the comic wall and a sample illustrated panel.

Moppa's Feral Chores Club

A proudly scruffy emu becomes an accidental ambassador for finishing jobs.

Moppa insists a mess is a creative project while sweeping it into the unmistakable shape of a second emu.

Setting & sample

A family activity day, junior club session or illustrated home edition.

A character sheet, a short strip and a chores activity page.

The Roadhouse Placemat Yarn

A one-meal comic, caption puzzle or small 'spot the forgotten item' scene that can be read while food arrives.

A traveller packed everything for the trip except the reason they stopped: the shopping list still pinned to the fridge at home.

Setting & sample

Roadhouse or club-meal tabletops.

A paper placemat with a comic and a missing-item puzzle arranged around the plate space.

The Shed Choir

A performed comedy piece builds a familiar shed soundtrack from voices, ordinary props and increasingly unhelpful commentary.

Solemn performers hold caption cards while one person supplies a tool noise and another disputes its accuracy.

Setting & sample

A hall gig or club variety night.

A short script, caption cards and an audio demo of the shed soundtrack.

The Town's Unofficial Useful Map

A fictional town mapped through unofficial landmarks: a shady seat, an awkward shop door and the bend where the recurring visitor takes the wrong turn.

A friendly illustrated town map with character vignettes and a recurring visitor getting increasingly overconfident.

Setting & sample

A fictional town in a book, show display or local publication concept.

An illustrated map of a fictional town, with its unofficial landmarks and recurring visitors.

One idea, different versions

01

The shared fridge

  • Poster: a cutaway fridge giving evidence about an ownerless casserole and somebody else's missing milk.
  • Crib-room column: a letter from the fridge requesting that its occupants stop referring to the smell as 'a maintenance issue'.
  • Thirty-second skit: workers disown a mystery tub until somebody announces that the container is free to take home.

One situation with a visual version, a written voice and a performed exchange.

02

The club galah

  • Match-day cartoon: the galah loudly coaches from the boundary while facing the wrong oval.
  • Gear-return notice: the same galah beside a mountain of club equipment and an empty bag labelled with its name.
  • Merchandise: a cap or shirt showing the galah as 'Head of Encouragement and Avoiding the Washing-Up'.

The same fictional character in a cartoon, a club notice and a wearable joke.
