THE ROUGH AS GUTS PRODUCT SHED

Out of the shed

Books, posters, cartoons, skits and club characters with something useful to offer: more care, safer choices and more room to enjoy life together.

Explore the product rangeTry the weird stuff
Joyful responsible abundance.

A laugh that leaves something better behind.

Joyful responsible abundance is the direction. These format ideas explore how light satire could help someone recognise a habit, try a different response and enjoy the result. The person in the picture gets to learn, contribute and belong. The examples remain open to revision with the people who would use them.

01

Illustrated books and themed collections

The Book of Nearly Done

A character proudly opens the Ministry of Nearly Done: one washed plate, a returned drill case without its charger, a bag packed without tomorrow's clothes. Each chapter ends with the character finishing the handover properly. The grand office closes early because there is finally time to sit down together.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
A person sincerely wants to help, but keeps handing over the unfinished part of the job.
Possible setting
Gifts between family, friends or co-workers; bookshops, club stalls and roadhouse shelves.
Sample version
Explore a linked chapter showing the excuse, the change and the shared payoff.

A way forward

Agree what a finished job includes, then complete your part and check the handover.

What it makes room for

Other people can rely on the help, and everyone gets more time back.

To ask readers: Can a reader describe what the character does differently, without feeling they are the joke?

02

Pocket booklets, zines and fold-out guides

Feeding Yourself Includes the Kitchen

The cook lays out a magnificent sauce collection and asks which bottle counts as the vegetable. A mate helps them choose a meal, check the ingredients and follow the recipe. The last page shows them clearing up their own cooking mess, with tomorrow's meal no longer an archaeological question.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
A new cook has enthusiasm, six sauces and no idea what dinner actually requires.
Possible setting
Leaving-home gifts, camp welcome packs, libraries and shared households.
Sample version
Develop the meal sequence with clear illustrations and checked food-handling guidance for its setting.

A way forward

Plan a meal you can make, follow its instructions, ask when unsure and clean up afterwards.

What it makes room for

More independence, a usable kitchen and less work quietly passed to someone else.

To ask readers: Does the guide help a learner work out what to do, rather than just point out inexperience?

03

Posters and noticeboard series

The Walkway Would Like Its Job Back

A worker presents an absurdly elaborate route around their own stored gear. In the next scene, they move it to the designated place with the appropriate help and leave the access route clear. The final caption can take the mickey out of the unnecessary expedition, while the picture shows the change.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
Materials creep into a shared access route until walking through becomes an obstacle course.
Possible setting
Workshops, camp facilities, club storerooms and site noticeboards.
Sample version
Adapt the drawing to the actual access route, storage arrangements and site review.

A way forward

Use designated storage and raise obstructions that you cannot safely resolve through the site's process.

What it makes room for

People can move and work without inheriting an avoidable obstacle.

To ask readers: Can workers identify the intended safe action, and does it match their actual site?

04

Newsletter column and recurring email feature

The Crib Room Correspondent

The kettle submits a fictional resignation after its third empty shift. The cartoon then follows the last user checking the next person has what they need and leaving the bench ready. A later issue could show that same person thanking a mate who has done the handover for them.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
A shared facility gets treated as though the next person is a free maintenance service.
Possible setting
Workplace, camp, club, association and community newsletters.
Sample version
Try a column and picture pairing a familiar shared-space frustration with a considerate handover.

A way forward

Leave shared equipment and spaces ready for the next user, following their instructions.

What it makes room for

Ordinary care gets passed along instead of ordinary resentment.

To ask readers: What act of consideration stays with a reader after the kettle gag?

05

Magazine, seasonal annual or mini newspaper

Rough as Guts: The Good Sorts Issue

A fictional issue follows a family, a camp crew and a club through jobs they used to leave for someone else. Cartoons sit beside practical pages and a 'How I Stopped Doing That' yarn. The celebration is the repaired relationship, returned gear or enjoyable shared meal, with the old excuse available for a gentle callback.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
Different people learn useful things from one another, with enough honesty to admit the messy beginning.
Possible setting
Club reading piles, local shops, ag-show stalls and community publications.
Sample version
Explore an issue around a shared theme, with each contribution showing its own useful change.

A way forward

Share a habit you changed, how you learned it and what helped you keep going.

What it makes room for

Learning becomes something people can contribute to and enjoy together.

To ask readers: Does the mix leave readers with usable ideas and people worth learning alongside?

06

Single-panel newspaper cartoons and four-panel strips

Bush Telegraph: From Grumble to Report

A fictional pothole has a fan club, anniversary cake and an enormous comments section. A resident interrupts the ceremony to send a clear report through the responsible authority's actual channel. The strip ends with a reference number and a follow-up, leaving the joke on the endless commentary rather than promising an instant repair.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
A local problem has collected plenty of opinions but nobody has passed on the information needed to act on it.
Possible setting
Country newspapers, community news sheets, town newsletters and local websites.
Sample version
Pair a fictional strip with the checked reporting information for the place using it.

A way forward

Report the problem clearly through the appropriate channel, without putting yourself at risk to collect details.

What it makes room for

A shared frustration becomes information someone can respond to.

To ask readers: Can a reader find the relevant reporting route, and is the cartoon fair to the people doing the work?

07

Caption jokes, illustrated one-liners and share cards

The Last-Square Defence

Someone points proudly to a lonely corner of toilet paper: 'Didn't finish it.' The next picture gives them the much less dramatic role of fitting the new roll and checking the spare. Their self-important defence becomes a callback while the next person finds a bathroom that works.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
A person puts more effort into avoiding a shared job than the job requires.
Possible setting
Household guides, camp amenities, club notices and newsletter cartoons.
Sample version
Compare a silent before-and-after version with a short exchange between the users.

A way forward

Replace shared supplies when you use them up, or tell the responsible person when stock is missing.

What it makes room for

The next person is looked after without needing to chase you.

To ask readers: Does the change get as much attention as the excuse?

08

Comedy sketches, short scripts and performed video

A Quick Favour, Properly Asked

A mate asks for a ute and 'a couple of things', then reveals furniture for an entire house. Instead of turning the helper into a fool, the scene lets both pause, agree the actual load and time, and arrange the rest. The requester carries their share while the mate retires the imaginary removal-company uniform.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
A vague request for help quietly grows into a job the helper never agreed to.
Possible setting
Club nights, short video series, hall entertainment and guide companions.
Sample version
Read different versions of the conversation, keeping the honest agreement and helpful ending visible.

A way forward

Say what help you need, ask what the other person can offer and respect their answer.

What it makes room for

People can help willingly without a favour becoming a hidden obligation.

To ask readers: Does the scene offer words someone could use without making generosity look foolish?

09

Sticker sheets, labels, magnets and removable decals

Put It Where Future You Can Find It

A reusable label shows a character presenting an empty hook as 'the search headquarters'. A matching panel shows them putting their keys or club gear in the agreed place. The final image is a calm departure, with their former search party enjoying the morning off.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
Tomorrow's missing item was left somewhere imaginative today.
Possible setting
Households, camp rooms, club storerooms, welcome packs and gift bundles.
Sample version
Explore a label and matching guide panel around a genuinely useful place to return an item.

A way forward

Choose an agreed home for the item and return it after use.

What it makes room for

Less searching, fewer interrupted people and more time for the day itself.

To ask readers: Does the label help the habit happen where the item is actually used?

10

Task drawings and visual companions to reviewed safe work method statements

The Plan That Fits the Job

A discussion scene lets a worker question the mismatch instead of pretending to understand it. The responsible task team checks and corrects the material before it is used. Any light satire belongs in the familiar 'reckon it'll be right' excuse, while the reviewed drawings communicate the real job clearly.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
A worker notices that the picture or written sequence does not match the task in front of the team.
Possible setting
Task-specific work documents and site communication, with the responsible organisation and reviewers.
Sample version
Develop drawings against the actual source document and retain the task team's review and revision notes.
Safe Work Australia: SWMS guidance

Safe Work Australia's guidance describes SWMS for high risk construction work. This proposal is illustration support tied to the specific work and its review process.

A way forward

Follow the approved method and raise unclear instructions or changed conditions with the responsible person before proceeding.

What it makes room for

Speaking up becomes part of looking after the crew and understanding the task.

To ask readers: Does the responsible task team confirm that the sequence, equipment and depicted controls match this job?

11

Short animation, loops and recurring episodes

Dinner's Done. So Is the Washing-Up.

A proud reveal of one plate becomes a slow pan across every saucepan in the house. The cook sees the gap, shares a plan with the household and does their part of the clean-up. The closing shot puts them at the table with everyone else, rather than leaving another person in the kitchen.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
The cook declares dinner finished while the cooking mess is still waiting for somebody else.
Possible setting
Short-video channels, club screens, camp displays and guide companions.
Sample version
Compare a quiet visual reveal with a short reply, keeping the clean-up and shared rest in both endings.

A way forward

Treat cooking, clearing and cleaning as a shared job with an agreed division of work.

What it makes room for

Feeding people also leaves them a usable kitchen and time to be together.

To ask readers: Can viewers see who changes what, and does the ending share the enjoyment as well as the work?

12

Tea towels, mugs, aprons, shirts, calendars and shed prints

The Bags Made It to the Car

A reusable shopping bag shows an absurd ceremonial procession from the kitchen hook to the front door. A companion calendar picture finishes with the owner actually using the bag at the shops. The joke celebrates remembering, with room for the owner to laugh at the old empty-handed entrance.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
An everyday object can remind someone of a habit they want to build.
Possible setting
Gift stalls, club fundraisers, roadhouses, small shops and direct-order bundles.
Sample version
Try the image on the object involved and compare whether it is a useful reminder or just decoration.

A way forward

Put the things you need with the belongings you already take when leaving.

What it makes room for

Preparation becomes easier to remember and less dependent on somebody else's reminders.

To ask readers: Would the owner welcome this reminder, and can they connect it to an action they want to take?

13

Sports-club mascots, match-day cartoons and junior activity sheets

The Galah Who Gives a Hand

The galah arrives with a giant coaching board and discovers today's useful role is on the volunteer roster. It asks where it is needed, finishes that job and thanks the other helpers. Its next grand announcement is that everyone can enjoy the game because the set-up is actually done.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
An enthusiastic club character has plenty to say and learns to make its help count.
Possible setting
Footy, cricket, bowls and other sports clubs, with a character developed for the individual club.
Sample version
Explore the character in a match-day strip, a practical club notice and a volunteer thank-you.

A way forward

Check the roster, ask where help is needed and follow through on the job you accept.

What it makes room for

The load is shared, new helpers belong and more people get to enjoy the club.

To ask readers: Would members see a character worth joining in with, rather than a mascot rewarded for avoiding work?

14

Generic public-facing issue cartoons, campaign posters and explainer strips

The Great Not-My-Job Debate

A fictional Shed Parliament debates the untidy community hall until someone asks who can do what and which decision needs the responsible organisation. The group shares the work within its authority and follows the relevant public process for the rest. Satire of the grand speeches sits beside a checked explanation people can use.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
A shared problem attracts speeches and blame while the practical responsibilities remain unclear.
Possible setting
Community papers, public exhibitions, civic information projects and general public issue campaigns.
Sample version
Explore a fictional cartoon alongside the verified process and responsibilities for the actual issue.

A way forward

Clarify the facts and responsibilities, take the part you can and use the appropriate public channel for the rest.

What it makes room for

People can participate and cooperate without being left with cynicism or an invented promise.

To ask readers: Is the factual explanation accurate, and does the satire leave readers a constructive way to participate?

15

Serial comic panels and rotating community displays

The Gear Comes Home

A club bag issues an extravagant reunion invitation to its scattered contents. Across the instalments, borrowers check their cupboards, return the gear and tell the coordinator about missing or damaged items. The final panel shows the next group ready to play, with the former borrowers helping them pack.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
Shared equipment cannot do its next job while it is spread across everybody's houses.
Possible setting
Camp noticeboards, halls, club foyers, libraries and community shops.
Sample version
Explore a serial that follows the return and handover, with local return details supplied by the club.

A way forward

Return shared gear to the agreed place and tell its coordinator about any problem.

What it makes room for

The next person gets a fair turn, and the coordinator does less chasing.

To ask readers: Can readers find where and how to return the real items, and is asking for help made easy?

16

Illustrated readings, hall-night comedy and caption contests

The Picnic Expedition

A picture shows a ute packed as though the picnic might colonise another planet. The narrator discovers nobody has checked the meeting place or who is bringing lunch. A second image has the group agree the essentials and share the jobs, then spend the outing enjoying each other's company.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
Preparing for a shared outing becomes an impressive display of equipment without an agreed plan.
Possible setting
Country halls, bowls clubs, show events, libraries and club entertainment.
Sample version
Read alternative explanations over both pictures and ask what the group changed between them.

A way forward

Agree the plan, check what is needed and share preparations with the people taking part.

What it makes room for

Preparation serves the shared experience instead of consuming it.

To ask readers: Does the second picture offer something useful beyond 'pack less' or 'stop being silly'?

17

Short audio yarns, radio-ready sketches and narrated cartoons

The Conversation Gets a Second Person

A storyteller delivers the history of three cars and a disappointing gearbox before noticing their mate is still holding the first question. They stop, ask about the other person's day and listen. The old car story returns as a shared callback rather than taking over the whole conversation again.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
A person is so busy telling a story that their companion has not had room to join in.
Possible setting
Community-radio submissions, club audio, podcasts and narrated guide editions.
Sample version
Try the exchange aloud with different pauses and responses, leaving room for both voices.

A way forward

Ask a question, listen to the answer and make room for the other person's story.

What it makes room for

People feel included, and the conversation has more to offer both of them.

To ask readers: Can listeners hear the change in attention, without one character being treated as beneath the other?

18

Caption games, scenario cards and printable club activities

What Would Help Here?

A picture card shows a borrowed item returned incomplete or a shared kitchen left unusable. Players can supply an absurd excuse, then suggest a response that helps resolve it. A second picture lets the character put things right, with room to discuss which line would invite cooperation rather than start a fight.

Premise, setting & sample
The premise
A familiar mess can open a conversation about helping, responsibility and the way people speak to one another.
Possible setting
Club tables, family gatherings, camp recreation rooms and facilitated youth activities.
Sample version
Explore picture-and-response cards with intended participants, including how they would want a mate to raise the issue.

A way forward

Practise asking for a change clearly, listening and agreeing who will do what.

What it makes room for

A difficult subject can become a shared problem to solve with some warmth.

To ask readers: Does the activity make repair and respectful conversation easier, or turn into scoring points against someone?

COUNTRY, OUTBACK & A BIT SIDEWAYS

Open the other shed.

A few stranger concepts for ag shows, country halls, roadhouses, camps and clubs.

The Cubicle Gazette

A changing back-of-door cartoon about leaving the amenities ready for the next person.

A character gives a victory speech over the last lonely corner of a toilet roll, then gets on with replacing it. A following panel shows them washing and drying their hands before leaving.

A way forward

Leave the facilities usable, wash and dry your hands, and report missing supplies or problems.

What it makes room for

The next visitor is cared for, and ordinary hygiene becomes part of sharing a place.

Setting & sample

Country-show toilets, club bathrooms and camp amenities, with the venue's involvement.

Explore a readable amenities sheet with the venue's supply and reporting details.

The Returned Property Royal Show

Give returned, paired and properly handed-over belongings the extravagant honours of a show pavilion.

Two reunited gloves receive the Champion Pair ribbon. Their owner returns them to the kit bag, where the next volunteer finds what they need. The absent-minded beginning remains available for a gentle joke.

A way forward

Check your belongings for borrowed gear, return it and mention anything missing or damaged.

What it makes room for

Recognition goes to restoring shared resources, with less chasing and more gear available.

Setting & sample

An ag-show display or club foyer.

Try illustrated awards beside a real, clearly explained return point.

The Corrugated Comic

A walk-along story about making a shared space usable again.

The first panel gives an absurd grand tour around a door blocked by everybody's stored things. The crew agrees where the gear belongs, gets the appropriate help to move it and uses the doorway normally. Their grand expedition ends at the tea table.

A way forward

Agree shared access and storage arrangements, then keep those spaces usable.

What it makes room for

Cooperation creates room to move, work and enjoy the shed together.

Setting & sample

A show stand, shed exhibition or temporary community-art display.

Explore the story as a paper wall, with each panel showing a clear part of the change.

Moppa's Have-a-Go Club

A scruffy emu learns how to finish a job with help, practice and a bit of play.

Moppa calls the pile under the bed a 'very private collection'. A friend helps work out where things belong, Moppa joins in and they find the missing game. The last panel has them playing together in the space they cleared.

A way forward

Ask for help with an unfamiliar job, take part and practise a way to do it again.

What it makes room for

Learning brings independence and enjoyment without making a child's circumstances the joke.

Setting & sample

Family activities, junior club sessions and illustrated home editions.

Explore a short strip and activity page with the young people who would use it.

The Roadhouse Placemat Yarn

A comic puzzle follows the journey from a spectacularly untidy meal to a table the next family can use.

The family searches for its car keys among a mountain of wrappers as though leading an expedition. They gather their rubbish, use the venue's bins and return the reusable items where asked. Their keys emerge, and the next visitors get a clear place to sit.

A way forward

Gather your belongings and rubbish, and follow the venue's arrangements for clearing your place.

What it makes room for

Travellers leave a welcome for the people who follow them.

Setting & sample

Roadhouse and club-meal tabletops.

Explore a placemat story and puzzle with the venue's actual table and return arrangements.

The Pack-Up Chorus

A comic chorus of 'someone else' gradually turns into people naming and sharing the pack-up jobs.

Performers initially produce a magnificent harmony of excuses. Then each takes an agreed role, using harmless stage props and voices. The final refrain happens with the pretend space ready, so the whole cast can take a bow together.

A way forward

Find out what needs doing, take an agreed role and let someone know when it is finished.

What it makes room for

Shared work leaves more people free to enjoy the event and each other's company.

Setting & sample

Hall gigs, club variety nights and community events.

Explore a short script with local volunteers and a clear transition from excuses to cooperation.

The Ask-a-Local Map

A welcoming map and comic let a visitor ask for help and a local practise giving useful directions.

The visitor is confidently heading for a landmark that moved years ago. They ask, the local replaces a long family-history explanation with clear directions, and both reach the community event. A real-place version would use checked local information.

A way forward

Ask when unsure, give clear information and check that the other person has what they need.

What it makes room for

Newcomers can belong, and local knowledge becomes something generously shared.

Setting & sample

A fictional town in a book, show display or community publication concept.

Explore a fictional map, then discuss a factual local version with the people represented.

One idea, different versions

01

The shared fridge becomes usable again

  • Poster: the mystery-tub joke leads to a worker labelling their own food and leaving the shelf and bench ready, following the agreed camp rules.
  • Crib-room column: a former fridge offender tells the story on themselves, explains the habit they changed and thanks the crew for making the arrangement clear.
  • Short skit: a person starts to deny responsibility, recognises their own container and puts it right. The final moment lets the crew enjoy lunch together instead of continuing the argument.

Each version keeps the recognition, the person's own response and the shared benefit. Food-handling and clear-out instructions need review for the actual setting.

02

The club galah helps everyone play

  • Match-day cartoon: the galah catches itself delivering an unnecessary grand speech, checks the volunteer roster and helps finish the set-up.
  • Gear-return notice: the galah returns the kit to the club's agreed point, reports a missing item and helps the next team find what it needs.
  • Merchandise: the galah's comic pride is in giving a hand and joining the game afterwards. The image celebrates contribution, with room to remember how it learned.

The mascot can be a learner and a good companion. Its recognisable humour need not reward avoiding the jobs that keep a club going.
