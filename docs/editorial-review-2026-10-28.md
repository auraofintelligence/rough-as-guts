# Rough as Guts: editorial review, 28 October 2026

The purpose is **joyful responsible abundance**: ethical light satire that helps people gain capability, care, wellbeing and room to enjoy life. A reader should be able to recognise a habit, see a useful alternative and understand why it is worth trying. The person is capable of learning. Their class, background, family or lack of previous instruction is not the joke.

The previous site often made the rough behaviour the most memorable and attractive part. Adding a general statement about competence did not repair a scene that ended with everybody admiring the mess. This review changes the actual scenes, endings, proposed actions and image briefs.

## What was reviewed

- The complete catalogue of Daz's 45 source slides, with closer review of the examples and counterexamples below.
- All nine current page objects, their chapters, gags, product concepts and rural ideas.
- The illustration manifest, featured source selection and generated brief form.
- Relevant primary guidance for the food-hygiene and workplace communication points retained in the new copy.

This is an editorial and source review. It is not a technical approval of the old posters, a field trial, or evidence that the revised material changes behaviour.

The pattern to carry forward is recognition, a different response and a valued benefit. The exact technical advice does not become reliable merely because that pattern is useful.

## Images and brief generation

Six replacement illustrations use the built-in image generator. The men's, women's and workplace heroes show participation and cooperation. Three comic sequences show a complete tool return, clothes being sorted before friends relax, and a teen checking and packing their own bag.

The [image record](../data/generated-art.json) retains prompts, file hashes, dimensions and the superseded assets. Older generated artwork remains as history; the original PowerPoint images remain unchanged. Visual inspection checked the depicted narrative and basic coherence. It does not establish technical safety approval or audience response.

The form keeps its simple structure. Its existing useful-point field becomes **Better action & why it helps**, and is required. The generated brief asks for a visible replacement behaviour, support where needed and a worthwhile payoff. In a single-panel image the better action should dominate; a sequence should not end at the risky or inconsiderate act.

## Grounding the practical details

Queensland Health explains that unsafe food can look, smell and taste normal, and advises checking use-by dates and storage. The fridge example therefore does not use a sniff as evidence of safety. [Queensland Health: signs of potentially unsafe food](https://www.qld.gov.au/health/staying-healthy/food-pantry/food-safety-for-consumers/food-safety-and-your-health/signs-of-potentially-unsafe-food).

Federation Council's home guidance separates pets and their bowls from kitchen food handling, calls for separate washing of pet items, and handwashing after touching pets. The women's example follows that direction without presenting domestic advice as a commercial-kitchen compliance assessment. [Federation Council: pets and food safety](https://www.federationcouncil.nsw.gov.au/Environment-Waste/Public-Health/Food-Safety-at-Home/Pets-Food-Safety).

WorkSafe Queensland's reporting guidance includes hazards, housekeeping concerns and damaged equipment, worker involvement, and acting on reports. The workplace copy gives the recipient of a concern a responsibility to listen and respond. [WorkSafe Queensland: reporting safety](https://www.worksafe.qld.gov.au/safety-and-prevention/creating-safe-work/reporting-safety).

Safe Work Australia describes identifying hazards, assessing and controlling risk and reviewing controls. The revised copy keeps work design, equipment and responsible management in view, rather than making worker attitude the whole explanation. [Safe Work Australia: identify, assess and control hazards](https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/safety-topic/managing-health-and-safety/identify-assess-and-control-hazards).

## What to learn from actual use

Ask intended readers what happened, what the person could try, who or what would support that change, and what the ending makes possible. Listen for unintended admiration of the harmful act, shame, confusion or a response that is not feasible in their circumstances.

For site use, the responsible task team must verify the actual depicted work and controls, resolve ambiguity, and involve the workers who will encounter the poster. Record their feedback and revise. Neither an AI-generated joke, an internal review nor a laugh on its own proves improved safety, wellbeing or behaviour.
